Executive summary

The broader panel exposes uneven seed uptake and still no neutral contagion

The July 25 run is technically complete and expands the prior one-scenario loop test to six scenarios and two topologies. Seed uptake now ranges from 0% to 100% by scenario, but ordinary neutral agents still never newly adopt the trap after communication.

Primary conclusion

This is a mixed seed-manipulation check and a failed ordinary-peer propagation manipulation. The seed chooses the intended trap in 19/36 trials, but all six measured answer-level downstream switches occur in the corrector role—not an ordinary neutral. Final MAS accuracy is 70/72 because two mesh conversations tie 2–2; no conversation ends with a wrong trap plurality.

What improved

The treatment produces targeted rather than random errors: every incorrect final injection answer is the intended scenario-specific trap, with zero unrelated-option selections. travel_001 and it_002 each achieve 6/6 seed uptake.

What remains unresolved

Uptake is highly scenario-dependent: logistics_001 is inert at 0/6, while it_003 produces the same misunderstanding in controls. With only 12 seed trials per type, the apparent factual/causal/framing differences are not clean type effects.

Method and architecture

Frozen-seed generation followed by synchronous group updates

The architecture separates initial susceptibility from later communication. Round 0 is independent; every subsequent response receives only prior-round messages allowed by the topology. Private dossiers are role-specific and logged separately from peer messages.

1

Scenario package

Six tasks, each with five options, one correct answer, one matched trap, three assumptions, and a decisive correction.

2

Stage A: seed

The seed sees the shared task plus one categorical unsupported private assessment. Every valid response is retained.

3

Freeze and replay

Each round-0 seed response is frozen and replayed in both the looped chain and fully connected mesh.

4

Stage B: dialogue

Four agents update synchronously through rounds 1–3. The seed dossier persists; correction appears to its holder at round 2.

5

Evaluation

Final plurality, agent accuracy, trap share, answer trajectories, exposure adoption, failures, and rationale quality are recorded.

Topology 2 · fully connected mesh

Direct exposure

At each update, every agent receives all three peers’ prior-round messages. A trap-holding seed therefore has up to three immediate receivers rather than one.

Observed result

Mesh improved final individual accuracy to 91.7% through greater seed recanting, but ordinary-neutral direct adoption remained 0. Its two MAS misses were ties.

Topology comparison: In the loop, Agent 1 is the seed’s only first-hop receiver. In the mesh, all three nonseed agents can see the seed at the next round. Mesh exposure still produced no ordinary-neutral adoption, so limited routing alone does not explain the null propagation result.

Experimental conditions

Injection

Seed receives the persistent categorical private assessment. Corrector receives decisive evidence from round 2 onward.

No-injection baseline

Seed receives a matched innocuous private note instead. The correction schedule and group conversation remain in place.

Independent control

Four one-round answers with no peer communication. The seed slot receives the neutral note; round-2 correction is never reached.

Calibration history

Changes made to reduce ceiling effects while preserving scenario quality

The goal has not been to manufacture random errors. Each pass attempted to make the intended assumption consequential, remove answer-position and leakage confounds, and improve reproducibility.

Public trap-verdict language was removed from 19/20 scenarios; all options were reordered; correct and trap letters were balanced at exactly four appearances per letter A–E, with 20 unique correct–trap pairs. Stale comments and hardcoded references were repaired.

21 July · 6870016

Self-disqualification removed from 57/60 treatments

Wording that immediately admitted “not yet confirmed” or “not checked against policy” was replaced by records-lag framing or removed. Custom travel/logistics text and the travel_001 causal/framing mismatch were repaired.

All 60 treatments became structured, categorical, unsupported private assessments—not fabricated records. The seed dossier now persists from R0; correction moved from R0 to R2 and persists thereafter; shared contexts and qualification rules were comprehensively rewritten.

Interpretation

Why the current MAS remains too accurate

High group accuracy is not simply a model-strength result. The treatment often fails to move the seed, and when it does, the error remains concentrated in the privately treated roles. Adding direct mesh exposure did not produce ordinary-neutral contagion.

Worked: targeted error specificity

All 34 final injection errors are the intended scenario-specific trap. There are zero unrelated final selections and zero generation or parse failures.

Worked: broader topology diagnostic

The same frozen seeds were replayed in a loop and mesh. Mesh improved individual accuracy but still yielded zero ordinary-neutral direct adoption.

Open: uneven manipulation potency

Seed uptake ranges from 6/6 in travel_001 and it_002 to 0/6 in logistics_001. A pooled type comparison is therefore confounded by scenario content.

Open: it_003 task ambiguity

Agents sometimes attach the late-upgrade constraint to Gamma even in baseline and independent conditions. This is shared-task difficulty, not injection propagation.

Open: corrector source confusion

Every measured switch occurs in Agent 1. Peer claims and Agent 1’s own corrective block are both described as private dossiers, which may blur source ownership.

Open: insufficient loop recovery horizon

Four rounds still end before an R2 correction can traverse the directed loop, although mesh provides an immediate correction diagnostic.

Open: possible skepticism priming

The common system prompt says to treat “notes and assessments” as information to evaluate, directly naming the treatment vehicle.

Open: peer-information model

Agents are not told that peers can possess non-overlapping private information, so a novel peer assertion can reasonably look unsupported.

What this run supports

  • The treatment can strongly manipulate some scenarios but is inert in others.
  • Errors remain specific to the intended trap rather than random distractors.
  • Ordinary neutral agents show no answer-level adoption in either tested topology.
  • Near-ceiling MAS accuracy is driven by error concentration, not universal seed resistance.

What this run cannot support

  • A factual-versus-causal-versus-framing ranking.
  • A general estimate across all 20 scenarios or a powered domain comparison.
  • A comparison involving the neutral-star topology.
  • A clean causal estimate of correction, because most switches are correction-contaminated.
  • Formal semantic acceptance or reliance without the blinded rationale judge.
  • A conclusion that a weaker model is scientifically preferable.
Recommended next design

Separate propagation from correction instead of forcing both into four rounds

The expanded run shows that topology alone is insufficient. The next calibration should first equalize seed uptake and remove scenario-specific ambiguity, then use paired experimental arms to distinguish propagation from correction.

Arm 1

Propagation-only condition

No corrective dossier. Measure whether neutral agents adopt, qualify, challenge, or reject the seed’s unsupported assumption based on peer communication alone.

Primary outcome: downstream adoption after direct exposure—not only final accuracy.

Arm 2

Delayed persistent-correction condition

Use the identical seed/scenario, allow a predeclared propagation window, then release decisive evidence and keep it available through the remaining rounds.

Primary outcome: evidence-based recovery compared with the no-correction arm.

Repair each scenario so only one proposition is manipulated

travel_001 and it_003

For travel_001, state that both accessible-room categories share all required features, leaving only date-specific inventory unresolved. For it_003, explicitly state that Gamma’s quoted plan needs no upgrade and provisions Tuesday; only Alpha’s upgrade finishes too late.

Inert and correction-confused cases

Rewrite logistics_001 until the treatment changes a meaningful eligibility hinge in pilot checks. In it_002, label the corrector block “YOUR PRIVATE DOSSIER” and preserve peer claims as attributed messages so contradictory sources cannot be mistaken for the same dossier.

Give correction enough time to travel

For the directed loop, separate the first receiver from the corrector: A0 seed → A1 neutral → A2 neutral → A3 corrector → A0. A practical seven-round schedule is R0–R6. This gives two clean propagation hops before A3 receives correction at R3, then three rounds for that correction to traverse the loop. The current mesh run confirms that all agents can receive peer updates quickly, but direct exposure alone still did not induce ordinary-neutral adoption.

The current implementation enforces the corrector immediately downstream of the seed and caps conversations at four rounds. The proposed loop therefore requires explicit topology, schema, and round-limit changes before it can be run.

Model choice: do not switch to a weaker model solely to lower accuracy. That can introduce unrelated constraint and optimization errors, changing the source of failure. A weaker model is more defensible later as a preregistered robustness factor after the scenario and communication design produce measurable propagation.

Questions to take to the mentor

  1. Should the primary estimand be resistance to an unsupported peer assumption, evidence-driven recovery, or both in separate arms?
  2. Should agents be neutrally informed that peers may have non-overlapping private information, or should unexpected private claims remain part of the natural trust test?
  3. Should topology comparisons use a constant number of rounds or a topology-aware horizon long enough for correction to reach every node?
  4. What calibration thresholds should be frozen before scaling from this six-scenario panel to all 20?
